PATIENT FAQ — LAB RESULTS

Note: Simplified educational content for training purposes only. Not medical advice.

Q: How long does it take to get my lab results?
A: Routine blood tests are typically available within 3–5 working days. Results are
reviewed by your ordering clinician before being released to you, so there may be a
short delay even after the lab has processed the sample.

Q: My HbA1c result came back higher than my target — what does this mean?
A: HbA1c reflects average blood glucose over roughly the past 2–3 months. A result
above target suggests glucose control may need adjustment, which could involve
lifestyle changes, a medication dose adjustment, or adding a new medication. Your
clinician will review the full picture (trend over time, other health factors) before
recommending next steps — a single elevated reading is not by itself a cause for alarm,
but should be discussed with your care team at your next follow-up or sooner if you're
concerned.

Q: Can I view my lab results before my clinician has reviewed them?
A: Routine results are held for clinician review before release, generally within 24–48
hours of the lab processing them, to ensure results are explained with appropriate
context. Urgent/critical results are flagged for expedited clinician contact directly,
rather than being released to the portal first.

Q: What should I do if I don't understand my results?
A: Contact the clinic to schedule a review call or message your care team through the
patient portal. Do not rely solely on general online reference ranges, as normal ranges
and clinical significance can vary by individual context, method used, and comorbid
conditions.
