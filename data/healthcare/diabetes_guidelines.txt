TYPE 2 DIABETES MANAGEMENT GUIDELINES — REGIONAL HEALTH AUTHORITY (SUMMARY)

Note: This is a simplified educational summary for training purposes only. It is not a
substitute for professional medical advice.

1. Diagnosis Thresholds
Type 2 diabetes is diagnosed when fasting plasma glucose is 126 mg/dL or higher on two
separate occasions, or HbA1c is 6.5% or higher, or random plasma glucose is 200 mg/dL
or higher with classic symptoms present. Prediabetes is defined as fasting glucose of
100–125 mg/dL or HbA1c of 5.7%–6.4%.

2. First-Line Treatment
Metformin is the recommended first-line pharmacological therapy for most newly
diagnosed patients, alongside lifestyle modification (diet, physical activity, weight
management). Initial dosing typically starts low and is titrated upward over several
weeks to reduce gastrointestinal side effects.

3. Monitoring Schedule
HbA1c should be checked every 3 months until stable at target, then every 6 months
once well-controlled. Target HbA1c is generally below 7% for most adults, though
targets may be individualised (e.g., relaxed to below 8% for older adults with
comorbidities, or tightened for younger patients early in diagnosis).

4. Lifestyle Recommendations
- At least 150 minutes per week of moderate-intensity physical activity
- Weight loss of 5–7% of body weight if overweight, shown to significantly improve
  glycaemic control
- Reduced intake of refined carbohydrates and sugary beverages
- Regular foot checks to monitor for peripheral neuropathy

5. Missed Dose Guidance (General, Non-Insulin Oral Medication)
If a dose of oral medication (such as metformin) is missed and it is close to the next
scheduled dose, the missed dose should generally be skipped rather than doubled up.
Patients should not take two doses at once to "catch up" without first consulting their
prescribing clinician, as this may increase risk of side effects.

6. When to Escalate Treatment
If HbA1c remains above target after 3 months on maximum tolerated metformin dose,
guidelines recommend adding a second agent (e.g., an SGLT2 inhibitor, GLP-1 receptor
agonist, or sulfonylurea) based on patient comorbidities, cardiovascular risk, and cost
considerations.
