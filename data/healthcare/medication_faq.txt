PATIENT HELPLINE — MEDICATION FAQ (SUMMARY)

Note: Simplified educational content for training purposes only. Not medical advice.

Q: What should I do if I miss a dose of my medication?
A: General guidance varies by medication. For most once-daily oral medications, if you
remember within a few hours of the scheduled time, take it as soon as you remember. If
it is almost time for your next dose, skip the missed dose — do not double up. Always
check the specific guidance for your medication or ask your pharmacist, as some
medications (e.g., blood thinners, insulin) have different rules.

Q: Is it safe to take two tablets at once to make up for a missed dose?
A: No — doubling up is not recommended for most medications without explicit guidance
from a prescribing clinician or pharmacist, as it can increase the risk of side effects
or overdose-related symptoms. Always consult before doubling any dose.

Q: Can I take my medication with food?
A: This depends on the specific medication. Some medications (e.g., metformin) are
better tolerated with food to reduce gastrointestinal side effects. Others require an
empty stomach for proper absorption. Check the patient information leaflet or ask your
pharmacist.

Q: What should I do if I experience side effects?
A: Mild side effects (e.g., mild nausea in the first weeks of a new medication) are
often expected and may subside. Persistent, severe, or unusual symptoms should be
reported to your prescribing clinician promptly. Severe allergic reactions (swelling,
difficulty breathing, widespread rash) require emergency care immediately.

Q: How do I know if my medication is interacting with another drug I'm taking?
A: Always inform every prescriber and your pharmacist of all medications, supplements,
and over-the-counter drugs you are taking. Pharmacists can run an interaction check at
the point of dispensing. Do not start a new over-the-counter medication or supplement
without checking for interactions first.
